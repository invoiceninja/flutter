# Detail screen layout

Companion to CLAUDE.md § Detail screens. The main file carries one rule per behaviour; this doc carries the layout itself, why each part is shaped the way it is, and what an entity has to supply to adopt it. Clients were the first screen on it (`lib/ui/features/clients/views/client_detail_screen.dart`) and every entity's record screen now is; the table under § Rolling this out to another entity says what each one does with each slot.

## The record layout

**A record screen runs identity, quick actions, standing, comments and the profile above a pinned tab strip — the same order at every width, with nothing behind a click.**

The screen it replaced showed a header that did nothing, a four-cell KPI strip that was half dashes, and up to seven cards in an order nobody chose. The order now, top to bottom:

| Slot | Widget | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | `EntityDetailHeader` (`subtitle`, `tags`, `nameMaxLines: 2`) | Who is this |
| Quick actions | `EntityQuickActions` | What do I most often do from here |
| Standing | `StandingCard` | Where do things stand |
| Comments | `EntityCommentsCard` (unchanged) | What did we last say about them |
| Profile | per-entity cards | Who to talk to, the details, the address, notes |
| Tabs | `EntityDetailTabs` via `layoutBuilder`, pinned | The records |

**The profile is always shown.** For one pass it sat behind a disclosure row — closed by default, remembered per entity type — with a one-contact summary card above it, so that the lists started higher in the narrow pane. Seen live, hiding an address behind a click read as a mistake and the row was removed at every width, along with the summary card and the preference that remembered the row's state. The cost is real and accepted: in the 440–560 px pane and on a phone the lists start below the profile's cards again. The strip still pins once reached.

Three widgets own the shell and take no `Services`, and one controller owns the plumbing:

- **`EntityRecordPage`** (`lib/ui/core/detail/entity_record_page.dart`) is the scroll view: the top in a `SliverToBoxAdapter`, the strip in a `PinnedHeaderSliver`, the active tab's body under it. One `CustomScrollView` on the `DetailScrollScope` controller, so the page keeps its single scrollbar and an embedded list still pages off this scroll. The strip is its own sliver for one reason — with a long embedded list it used to scroll away, and switching tab meant scrolling all the way back up first.
- **`EntityRecordColumn`** (`entity_record_column.dart`) lays out the slots. Below `Breakpoints.entityFormMultiColumn` (1000) it is one `CenteredFormColumn` stack; at or above it the header and quick actions sit beside the standing card (flex 11 : 9) and everything else runs full width. **There is no left rail and no second layout** — the same order at every width, so nothing has to be re-learned between the pane and full screen. **The two sides of that band end on one line**, whichever is taller: the band (`_EvenBand`) lays each side out at its own height and then lays the shorter one out again at the taller one's, so the standing card stretches down level with the tiles (its figures centred), or the tiles drop to the foot of a taller card. As a top-aligned `Row` the shorter side simply stopped short — the Balance card ended 26 px above the tiles on a client with no credit. It is a render object because `IntrinsicHeight` cannot do it: both sides contain a `LayoutBuilder`, which has no intrinsic height.
- **`EntityDetailTabs.layoutBuilder`** is what lets one `TabController` drive two slivers: the tabs widget *wraps* the page and hands back `(strip, body)` for it to place. That inversion means the default page reveal has nothing to find, so the host passes an `EntityRecordPageController` and wires `EntityDetailTabs.onReveal` to `revealTabs()`. `revealTabs` scrolls only when the strip is not already pinned *and* less than 160 px of body is showing — without the second half, a figure that opens its tab would change the tab under the user's thumb with none of its content on screen.

- **`RecordScreenController`** (`record_screen_controller.dart`) is everything a host would otherwise re-derive, because each piece carries a rule that is invisible at the call site and was learned the hard way on the client screen: the page, tab-selection and list-filter controllers; the quiet re-check on open, **decided when its timer fires** (a record screen outlives a company switch); the tab counts, kicked only **after** that re-check settles and built from the **record's** id, never the route's; and `refresh`, which reloads page 1 of the list on stage through the page's signal and never the list's own `refresh`. A screen builds one in `initState`, calls `attach(recordId:, revision:, tabIds:)` from its body builder, hands `buildPage` to `EntityDetailTabs.layoutBuilder`, and disposes it. What a host adds on top — the client's past-due line, a vendor's spend — hangs off `onSettled`, `refreshAfter` and `onBackOnline`. `RecordScreenController.withEnvironment` takes the five things it reads off `Services` one by one, which is what lets `record_screen_controller_test.dart` drive the timing without an app behind it.

**The gaps are owned by whoever can be empty.** Quick actions (no action applies), the comments card (nothing written) and the standing footnote (figure not known) can each turn out to draw nothing *after* the column has laid them out, so each carries its own trailing or leading gap and the column adds none. This is `docs/comments-and-activity.md` § A card that hides itself still costs a gap, applied three more times.

**On a wide window the profile's lead cards share a row.** `ClientDetailProfile` puts Contacts · Details · Address side by side as equal cards (as many of the three as have content), with Payment Methods and Notes full width beneath; below 1000 px the same cards stack in that order. Contacts is a card *in that row* on purpose: as a strip across the whole window each contact's copy and open-portal buttons sat at the far edge — on a 1,780 px window, about 1,400 px from the name they act on — and in a card a third as wide they cannot be far from it. One `_Sections` computation decides which cards exist, so an entry that would draw nothing is not given a gap or a column.

`RecordProfileLayout` (`record_profile_layout.dart`) is that arrangement for everyone else: `lead` cards in level rows of at most three (four are two and two, never three and a stray), `tail` cards full width beneath, one stack below 1000 px. Two rules it cannot enforce: **pass only cards that will draw something** (the gap is paid per entry), and **a lead card may not contain a `LayoutBuilder`** — the row is levelled with `IntrinsicHeight`, which a `LayoutBuilder` cannot answer; that is why notes, which clamp with one, are always a tail card.

**A lead card with the row to itself takes the width, and runs its rows in two columns.** A sparse record — a task, a vendor with nothing but a currency — has one card, and this case was built three ways on three screens before it was one: held to the header's column with a blank under the standing card, two columns decided by the host, and a single column against a window of empty card. Whoever places the card wraps it in `DetailRowColumnsScope(columns: 2)`, and a card that draws its rows through `DetailRowColumns` (`lib/ui/core/widgets/detail_row_columns.dart`) splits them — the card measures nothing, so it stays legal in the level row. Fewer than four rows stay one column: two rows side by side read as a table with a missing header.

Once the header has scrolled away, the scaffold's fixed bar shows `compactTitleForItem` — the name and the one figure that matters — so a long invoice list still says whose it is. It fades in past `kDetailCompactTitleRevealOffset` (72 px) and tapping it scrolls to the top. How it shares the bar depends on the form the action cluster takes. **Beside the spread bar** (a wide window) its width is reserved whether or not it is showing: the spread bar counts how many buttons fit the width it is given, so a title that only took room while visible would reshuffle them each time the user scrolled past the threshold. **Beside the compact cluster** (a pane or a phone — one primary button and `⋮`, a fixed size) the cluster is laid out first, at its own width, and the title gets what is left, or is not offered below 120 px. The reservation used to apply here too, sized for a primary that reads "Edit"; an invoice's reads "Enter Payment", and on a 360 px phone at large text the reserve pushed it off the bar. The cluster is told its form through `ActionBarLayoutScope` rather than asked to measure a width it is not being given, and its primary shortens to an ellipsis on a bar narrower than the cluster itself, so `⋮` — which holds every other action — never leaves the edge.

## A quick-action tile is a second render of an existing action

**A quick-action tile is a second render of an existing `EntityActionItem`, never a second definition — and one that does not apply to this record yields its slot to the next.**

`EntityQuickAction<A>` wraps an item with the three things a tile needs that a menu row does not: a `shortLabel` that fits ~70 px in every locale (the entity noun — "New Invoice" is "Nieuwe factuur"; the full label goes to the tooltip and the screen reader), an `applies` rule, and an optional `busy` listenable. Gating, the confirmation prompt (`guardedOnTap`) and the unsynced guard all stay on the item, so a tile cannot offer what the menu would refuse.

`applies` is not `enabled`. *Enabled* says whether the action can run at all; *applies* says whether it is worth a tile for this record right now — Enter Payment only while something is owed, Call only when there is a number, View Statement only once there is history. An action that does not apply stays in the `⋮` menu. The host supplies a priority list and `pickQuickActions` takes the first four that apply (six once the strip itself is ≥ 560 px wide), so the strip is always full of things that work rather than a fixed set with holes.

For clients: New Invoice, Enter Payment, Email, Call, View Statement, Client Portal, New Quote, New Task, New Project. Create tiles — and the Create New submenu — gate on `createPermissionFor(type)` as well as the module; before this the menu offered New Invoice to a user who may not create one. A deleted or unsynced client gets no tiles at all: the state banner already says why, and four tiles that would each answer "sync first" are four dead ends.

Tiles sit on `surface` with the card border and `shadow1`, not `surfaceAlt` (1.04–1.22:1 against the page in every palette — it does not read as something to press), and are neutral rather than accent-tinted, because the accent is the user's own choice and a red or green one would read as overdue or paid. The label scales down (`FittedBox`) rather than wrapping; `IntrinsicHeight` + stretch keeps the row's bottom edge straight when one label scaled.

**Deliberately not done:** the tiled actions were *not* removed from the fixed action bar's inline spread. On a wide window that leaves View Statement and Client Portal visible twice. The bar is what remains on screen once the tiles scroll away, and forcing an always-present "More" into `EntityOverflowActionBar` means putting a `MenuAnchor` among `OverflowView`'s measured children, which that widget's own comments warn against. Revisit if the duplication reads as clutter in use.

## Every value is an action

A value the user can act on does the obvious thing when tapped, and stays copyable:

- **`StandingFigure.onTap`** opens the tab that lists what the figure adds up; the amount is still copied by the hover icon or a long-press (`_FigureTarget` — the split `DetailInfoRow` already uses for a row with its own tap).
- **Email is text, not a link.** It copies; writing to it is the Email action. Making the address launch a mail client would put two small links two pixels apart (the phone under it dials) and take away the one thing a desktop webmail user wants from an address. `composeEmail` (`lib/ui/core/utils/mail_actions.dart`) builds `mailto:` from `cleanEmailAddress` — a strict single-address check that refuses `?`, `,`, `;`, `%`, whitespace, double quotes and angle brackets, because a contact's email is user data and `mailto:a@b.c?bcc=…` is a header-injection shape. It does accept what real addresses use: `+`, an apostrophe, letters with combining marks, and `&` in the local part, which goes out as `%26`. On a failed launch it copies the address and says so; **on web it copies as well as launching**, because the browser launcher reports success whether or not anything handled the link and a webmail user would otherwise get nothing. The manifest's `mailto` entry in `<queries>` is belt-and-braces (`android_url_queries_test`); nothing gates on `canLaunchUrl`.
- **`AddressBlock`** is one copyable block with a View Map link (`mapSearchUri`: Apple Maps on Apple platforms, Google elsewhere). A shipping address identical to the billing one — compared as displayed — is one block titled with both labels, rather than a second card that makes the user compare them.
- **`PartyContactRow`** (`lib/ui/core/widgets/party_contact_row.dart`) replaced a `Wrap` of four 32 px text buttons per contact. The first two actions that are available become icon buttons at the touch floor; the rest go behind `⋮`. It takes plain strings and callbacks because `Contact` and `VendorContact` share no supertype. The portal copy is labelled `Client Portal: Copy Link`, since the record's own menu has a different "Copy Link".
- Links are `accentInk` with `underlineAtRest: linkNeedsAtRestCue` (underlined at rest on touch, where there is no hover to reveal them), and muted labels on a card are `ink2`: `ink3` is 3.98:1 on white in the default light theme and fails 4.5:1 in five of six palettes; `ink2` is 9.47:1.

The Details card also shows what the edit screen can set and the old view hid: payment terms, group, assigned user, industry, size, task rate, valid VAT, send reminders, quote valid-until, and created / updated. `hasContent` is derived from the same row list `build` renders (`rowsFor`), which fixed a titled card with no rows — it used to count custom values that have no configured label. (Once the formatter has loaded the card always has its Created / Updated rows, so for a loaded record Details is never an empty section; the gate matters before that.) The task rate goes through `PartyCurrencyBuilder` like the standing card: formatted with the client's own currency id alone, a group-inheriting client's rate read in the company's currency beside a balance in the group's. A contact whose only entry is a custom value the company has labelled is shown — `visibleClientContacts(contacts, company:)` — since the row now prints those.

## A derived figure is shown only when it is proven

**A figure the server does not supply is shown only when it is proven — the client's past-due line is withheld unless a complete fetch and the client's own balance agree.**

The server has no per-client "overdue" figure. `clientPastDue` (`lib/domain/clients/client_past_due.dart`, pure — `today` is a parameter) works it out from locally cached invoices and returns **null unless all of these hold**:

1. `fetchComplete` — the screen just asked for this client's Sent and Partial invoices (`InvoiceRepository.ensureUnpaidForClientLoaded`: `client_id`, `client_status=unpaid`, `status=active,archived`) and the answer was one page the server did not fill.
2. Nothing in play is mid-edit — a clean client row, no dirty or `tmp_` invoice.
3. **The `Decimal` sum of those invoices' balances equals `client.balance`.** The server defines the balance as exactly that sum (`ClientService::calculateBalance`), archived invoices included, so a disagreement means something is stale and there is no telling which side.

(1) and (3) cover each other. The sum check alone can be fooled: with equal monthly invoices, a stale "unpaid" March and an uncached April add up to the right balance and would call March late. The fetch brings April in, the sums then disagree, and the figure is withheld. The fetch alone cannot remove a locally cached invoice that has since been paid — it is simply absent from "unpaid", so its old row stays — and the sum check catches that. `test/domain/clients/client_past_due_test.dart` has a case for each.

**The amount is what is late, not what is owed on late invoices.** An invoice can ask for a deposit ahead of its own due date, and it is past due the day the deposit is — but only the deposit is. `lateAmountOf` counts `min(partial, balance)` until the invoice's own due date has passed and the whole balance after; counting the whole balance printed "Past Due: $10,000.00" for a $1,000 deposit a week overdue, and passed every proof check while doing it. The count is still of invoices, so the amount can be less than the red balances in the list the line opens.

The three renderings are the rule: **late** → amount and invoice count in the overdue ink, linked; **proven, nothing late** → a zero in neutral ink (under a balance it answers the question the balance raises); **not known** → no line at all, and no gap for one. Balance itself is plain ink. It used to turn red whenever it was above zero, which made a client invoiced yesterday on thirty-day terms look delinquent, in the red the invoice screen uses for *past due*.

The line is label–value pairs ("Past Due: $1,240.00 · Invoices: 2") rather than a sentence because the bundles have no plural forms, and it uses `past_due`, never `overdue` — the French translation of `overdue` reads "unpaid".

Tapping it sends a `ListFilterIntent` (`{'overdue': {'true'}}`, widened to archived only when a late invoice is archived) through `EmbeddedListIntents` and selects the Invoices tab. The list applies it with the same `applyDeepLinkIntent` a dashboard panel's "View All" uses, so the user lands on exactly the invoices that were counted, behind a chip they can see and remove. The mailbox exists because the screen has no handle on the list: it sits under the tabs and may be unmounted, or mounted and offstage.

`ClientPastDueViewModel` gathers the inputs. It does nothing for a client that owes nothing, for an unsynced one, or with the invoices module off. It asks again when the record it asked about has moved — keyed on **balance and `updatedAt` together**, because the same balance can be made of different invoices — and bumps a generation when it does, so an answer still on the wire about the record as it was is not taken as an answer about it as it is. An ask that failed is made again when the device comes back online (`Services.backOnline`).

`Invoice.isPastDueOn(Date)` was added for this; `isPastDue` is now `isPastDueOn(Date.today())`.

**A sum of related rows is shown only when the device holds all of them — `RelatedRowsProof`.** The same problem arrives wherever a figure is added up from rows the server keeps no total for: what was spent with a vendor, the hours logged and the amount not yet invoiced on a project. Those rows are paged in fifty at a time, so the local table may hold some of them, and the old cards summed whatever was there. `RelatedRowsProof<T>` (`lib/ui/core/detail/related_rows_proof.dart`) hands the rows over (`rows`) only when they are all of them, using the number the screen already has — the tab's server count:

- no count known at all (never asked, or asked and unanswered): the rows held are handed over, as before;
- the count says more than the device holds: `rows` is null, the missing pages are fetched **scoped to the record** (the tab's own request, so it neither reads nor moves the sync cursor), and the rows come back complete;
- more than twenty pages: withheld and not fetched for — that is a report, not a record screen;
- a count from earlier in the session and no connection to complete against: withheld until back online. A known shortfall is not shown as a total because the device happens to be offline.

"Complete" is latched against the count it was proven for, so archiving a row from the tab below (against a count taken before it) does not blank the figure; a *different* count is a new question. A row created offline is not in the server's count and is not counted toward it. **The watch, the count and the fetch must mean the same rows** — all active-only, all under what the tab's own list sends — or holding as many as the server counted proves nothing. That is why a vendor's expense count and a project's task count carry `without_deleted_clients=true`, and why `ExpenseDao.watchForVendor` and `TaskDao.watchForProject` take `activeClientsOnly`: without it the watch still returned rows billed to an archived client, which could reach the count with rows the count does not include and call an incomplete set complete. (`BaseEntityDao.clientActiveFilter` is the server's predicate; the lists' own `clientNotDeletedFilter` drops deleted clients only, which is a looser, older rule the proofs cannot use.) **And the user has to be allowed to see every row**: without `view_expense` / `view_task` the server lists — and counts — only a user's own, the proof passes, and the total would be theirs under the vendor's or the project's name. Both screens draw nothing derived for such a user. While rows are withheld a vendor's two figures are blank, and a project falls back to the server's own running total (`current_hours`, whole hours) with no money figure — the same card it shows a user who may not view tasks. `held` is the rows whether or not they are all of them, for questions that may be answered from part of the list ("is there anything here to invoice?").

## Tab counts are server totals, and unknown is not zero

**The counts on a record's related tabs are server totals (`BaseEntityApi.count`), and a count that is not known draws no badge — never a zero.**

A client's Invoices tab pages its rows in fifty at a time, so the local table knows how many it has *fetched*, not how many there *are*. That is the opposite trade from the sidebar's counters (`docs/entity-lists.md` § Sidebar counters), which are local, never issue a request, and can under-report. A tab badge that under-reported would be read as the size of the list the user is about to open, so these ask.

`ApiClient.getListTotal` requests `per_page=1` with the tab's own starting filters (`client_id`, `status=active`) and reads `meta.pagination.total`. It reads nothing into Drift and is not `getList`, so it takes no sync cursor and returns none for a caller to store by mistake. The base `QueryFilters::client_id` applies to every table with that column, which is what makes one filter map correct for all eight tabs.

`RelatedTabCountsViewModel` (`lib/ui/core/detail/related_tab_counts.dart`) owns one request per enabled tab, and **rations them** — eight per record opened adds up against a hosted limit of 1,000 requests a minute per token *and* per IP (`RouteServiceProvider.php`), which an office shares:

- the host kicks it only **after its own re-check of the record has settled**. Kicked beside it, a re-check that found a newer row bumped `updatedAt` mid-flight and every count was asked for twice;
- a 1 s debounce, so stepping down a list with J / K fires none;
- at most three on the wire at once;
- a count taken for this `updatedAt` within the last 60 s is not asked for again — the session cache on `Services.relatedTabCounts` records when and for which revision each was taken, and also gives a re-opened record its badges in the first frame;
- a refresh is single-flight, and files its answers under the revision current when they land, so the newer record it usually arrives with does not make them look stale.

Offline it asks nothing and keeps what is cached; what went unanswered is asked when the device comes back online. The screen builds it — and the past-due view model — from the **record's** id once that is a server id, never from the route's: a screen opened on a client created offline keeps its `tmp_…` route after the client syncs. The cache is scoped by user as well as company, cleared in `onBeforeLogout` (the hook every session end passes through) and again in `onSessionReset`, and guarded by a generation so an answer landing after a clear is not written back; an answer that lands after a company switch is dropped. One tab's count failing costs the others nothing and shows no error.

A real zero *is* drawn (muted): it is a tab not worth opening. Locations, a payment's Invoices, a task's Time Log and Documents count from the record itself and need no request — Documents through `buildStandardDocumentsTab`, which used to write the number into the label ("Documents (2)") and leaves the badge off at none, an empty Documents tab being the usual case rather than news.

**The count is asked with the filter the tab's own list sends, and that is not one key.** The server names a parent differently on different lists: a project is `project_id` on invoices, `project_tasks` on tasks and `project_ids` on expenses, and has no filter at all on quotes — so `RecordScreenController.countFilterKeys` maps a tab to its key, and the project's Quotes tab is not counted (the server would answer, with every quote the company has). A bank account's transactions are `bank_integration_ids`, plural; the singular is silently ignored. A count taken with any other key is not the number of rows the tab will show.

**…and with the parameters its repository adds on its own** (`RecordScreenController.countExtras`). Most client-bearing lists send `without_deleted_clients=true` unless they are scoped to a client (`excludeDeletedClients` in `ensurePageLoadedTemplate`) — which the server reads as "the client is neither deleted **nor archived**" — and the transactions list always sends `active_banks=true`. No screen sees either, so the first cut of the project's three counts and the bank account's one went out without them: a project whose client had been archived wore a Tasks badge over a list that fetches nothing, and an archived bank account the same. A client's own tabs need none — client scope suppresses the parameter.

**Counts the session already holds are announced when the re-check settles.** `RelatedTabCountsViewModel.kick` adopts cached counts without notifying, because it was written to run inside build, where the tabs are built straight afterwards. The controller's settle-time kick is not build. Without a `notifyListeners()` after it, a record re-opened with its counts cached showed no badges until something else rebuilt the strip — which the re-check's own write usually did online, and nothing did offline or when the counts came back unchanged.

**Not counted:** Comments. The activity feed is a capped scan window that is filtered afterwards, so a count taken from it would under-report on a busy record.

## Record states: one banner, and unreachable is not missing

**A deleted record is read-only through one banner and `EmbeddedListParentScope`; a record that could not be fetched says so and offers Retry — it is not "not found".**

- **Banner.** `EntityDetailScaffold.bannerForItem` pins a strip above the scroll view, built by `entityStateBanner`: the not-synced notice, then *deleted* ("This record is deleted. Restore it to make changes.") or *archived* (the date), each with Restore — the same action item the menu offers, through the same confirmation gate. `ClientActions.itemsFor` gates archive, restore and delete on `edit_client` (the server authorizes all three through the edit policy, and invoices already gated them this way), so a view-only user gets the banner without the button. The header's state pill is dropped while a banner shows; the word once is enough. **A host that passes `bannerForItem` gets the same widget shape whether or not a banner is showing** (`Column[?banner, Expanded(body)]`): returning the body bare when there was none changed the widget under it the moment a record was archived or restored, which remounted the page — back to the top, every tab's filter, search and selection gone.
- **Read-only.** `isReadOnly` gates the `E` shortcut. A deleted client gets no quick tiles, no note buttons (`EntityNoteActions.none`), and its Documents and Locations tabs list what is there and offer nothing else — Documents keeps **View**, which the old read-only mode took away along with Delete. Archived is not read-only: the server accepts edits and new records for an archived client.
- **Embedded lists.** A related list takes a `clientId` and nothing else, so it could not know its parent was deleted (it offered New) or unsynced (New built a document pointing at a `tmp_` id). `EmbeddedListParentScope` says both once instead of threading two parameters through eight list screens. It also fixed the `N` shortcut, which ignored the parent-prefilled create handler and started a record for nobody.
- **Unreachable.** `ensureLoadedTemplate` returns an `EnsureLoadedOutcome` (cached, fetched, missing, unreachable, failed, skipped), and **every repository's `ensureLoaded` is declared `Future<EnsureLoadedOutcome>`**. The scaffold shows "No internet connection" or the generic error with **Retry** (beside the host's own `emptyAction`, not instead of it) for the retryable ones, and keeps "not found" for when the server actually said so — and once the record has been seen, a later disappearance is "not found", never the old outcome. Before this, opening a good link offline told the user the record did not exist. Every detail screen gets it — `hydrate` is typed `Future<Object?>`, so a host that returns nothing behaves as before. *The declared type is not a nicety.* For a while the wrappers stayed `Future<void>` and carried the outcome at runtime only; `ensureLoaded().timeout(…, onTimeout: () {})` in the Log Call party lookup then failed Dart's runtime check of `onTimeout` against the future's real type argument, the surrounding `catch` swallowed it, and the sheet opened with no contacts. Nothing in the analyzer or the suite could see it.
- **An unsynced parent fetches nothing.** `ensurePageLoadedTemplate` returns early when a parent-scope filter names only `tmp_` ids, and the email-history view model has the same guard: no server record can point at an id the server has never seen, and asking produced "Failed to load" over what is simply an empty list.

## Refresh

A detail screen had no way to refresh. Opening a cached record makes no request (`ensureLoaded` returns as soon as it finds the row), so a balance that changed on the server stayed stale until the next sync.

- **Pull-to-refresh** on `EntityRecordPage` (`AlwaysScrollableScrollPhysics`, because the page is handed a controller — `docs/pull-to-refresh.md`), and **`R`** on the scaffold.
- One refresh (`RecordScreenController.refresh`) covers the record (`refreshByIds`, dirty-preserving), the activity feed, then whatever depends on the record being current (the past-due inputs, a withheld sum) and the tab counts, and — through `DetailRefreshScope` — the embedded list **on stage**. The other lists are mounted but offstage; eight lists re-fetching for one pull is seven requests nobody asked for.
- **The list reloads page 1 of its own scope — `GenericListViewModel.reloadFirstPage`, never `refresh`.** `refresh` is `refreshAll`: every page of the entity, every state, company-wide. Wired to the record's refresh it turned a pull on one client into a download of every invoice the company has.
- **A quiet re-check on open**, 300 ms after the screen mounts, so the figures are not only as fresh as the last sync. It runs only for a record that is on the server, is held locally **for this screen's company**, and while that company is still the active one — a record screen outlives a company switch, and asked then it would be requesting another company's record under the new company's token. The server refuses that with a 401, which used to end the session; that is fixed at the root (`docs/sync.md` § A permission denial is a 401 too), and the guards are there so the request is not made for nothing.
- `R` does not repeat while held, and a second press before the first refresh lands joins it.
- **Not while offline.** The by-id request would only fail, and `refreshByIdsTemplate` logs every failure at WARNING — one line in the diagnostics log for each cached record opened without a connection, which is an ordinary thing to do here. The screen still settles, so cached counts still show.
- **Every browsable entity re-fetches by id.** `BankTransactionRepository` and `BankAccountRepository` had no `refreshByIds` override — the base is a no-op — so their screens' refresh, their re-check on open and the Outbox's Check all fetched nothing: a transaction matched on another device stayed Unmatched here until the next sync. The three settings-hosted records (expense category, gateway, payment link) are bundled reference data with no by-id fetch; their pull runs the repository's own `refreshAll`, the delta of a table that is a handful of rows.

There is no Refresh item in the `⋮` menu yet; on a pointer platform `R` and the browser-style pull are the two ways in.

## Tabs

- A tab has an **`id`** (`DetailTabIds`) as well as an index. `TabSelectionController.selectId` is how a figure opens "the Invoices tab" without knowing which modules are on; `select(index)` stays for the two leading tabs and View All, which `comments_surface_wiring_test` pins. The last tab is remembered by id, so a module switched off between visits cannot land the user on the wrong tab.
- **`[` and `]`** step through the strip. They live in the scaffold's fixed shortcut map beside `E`, not in the customisable catalog, which holds global shortcuts only — and they are bound **by character as well as by key**, because on a German, French or Nordic layout a bracket is typed with Option or AltGr and a `SingleActivator` accepts neither. The scaffold has its own focus owner (`docs/keyboard.md` § A record screen's own keys are a fixed map, not catalog entries), without which none of its keys were heard until the user clicked into the record. The strip is one tab stop with arrow / Home / End roving; focus stays on it when the tab changes by a click or `]` too, and an arrow with ⌘ / Alt / Ctrl held is left for history back.
- **A tab's body is keyed** (by its id, else its index). The stack holds only the tabs opened so far, so opening an *earlier* one moves every later body down a slot; unkeyed, Flutter matched them by position, handed the Invoices body's element to Comments, and built Invoices again from nothing — filter, search, selection and scroll gone, page 1 fetched again. Every record screen lands on its third tab, so the comments card's View All, `[`, and any tap to the left of the landing tab did it. It predates the rollout; `entity_detail_tabs_test.dart` pins it.
- A tab that is not showing is `ExcludeFocus`'d as well as `Offstage`: `Offstage` hides a body without taking focus from it, so on native touch a field in the tab just left kept the keyboard, and on desktop every tab ever opened stayed in the Tab order. This applies to every screen that uses `EntityDetailTabs`.
- A strip longer than its slot gets an **all-tabs button** on its trailing edge (a menu with a pointer, a bottom sheet on touch — fifteen menu rows run off a phone). See `docs/pane-width-and-overflow.md` § A tab strip is a width budget, and Material spends 52 px first for why it is an overlay rather than a sibling.

Where a screen departs from the client's, and why:

- **The five billing documents keep the PDF beside the record** (`BillingDocRecordBody`, from 900 px), so the record column is 5/11 of the window — a single stack on almost every display; the level band appears from about 2,200 px. Their header is one widget for all five (`BillingDocRecordHeader`), with the type as an overline and the status pill and party label as slots: the `Viewed` link has to be mounted where its reveal controller lives, and a cross-entity name link is allowed only in a named list of host files. The public notes, terms and footer stay on the Overview tab under the totals — in the pane, a paragraph of boilerplate terms above the line items pushed the document off the screen — so the profile carries Private Notes only.
- **An expense's subtitle is icon-led entries, not dotted segments.** Four heterogeneous names in a row (vendor, client, category, date) do not say which is which, and a dotted line that wraps leaves a dangling dot at the end of its first line at 480 px.
- **A transaction has no tabs**: nothing hangs off it. Matching is its job, so the match panel is a card in the profile — above Details on a narrow screen, beside it on a wide one — and is not drawn for a deleted transaction.
- **The three settings-hosted records** go through `SettingsRecordBody` (`lib/ui/core/detail/settings_record_body.dart`), which keeps the `SettingsFormShell` width cap every screen under `/settings/…` has while scrolling on the scaffold's controller, so the compact title and pull-to-refresh work there too.
- **A recurring expense has no comments card.** The server stores a note on one and has no feed to read it back from — `BACKEND.md`.
- **Applying a payment asks first.** The Apply tile used to allocate the unapplied amount to the client's oldest unpaid invoice on one tap, to an invoice the user was never shown, and an allocation cannot be undone from the app. It now names the invoice, what is owed on it and what will be applied (`applyPaymentToOldestInvoice`), Cancel focused.

**Permissions came with the tiles.** A tile puts an action one tap away, so the action lists were brought closer to what the server enforces. These gates are on the items, so they reach list rows and edit screens as well:

- **Changing a record is `AuthSession.canEditRecord`, never `can('edit_<entity>')` alone.** The server's rule (`EntityPolicy::edit`) is an admin, a holder of `edit_<entity>`, **or the record's creator or assignee** — and a user without `view_<entity>` is listed only the records they created or are assigned (`BaseController`'s list scope). So for the commonest restricted user ("may create tasks, nothing else") the third arm is the only one that ever applies. The first cut of these gates checked the permission alone, which took Archive, Restore and Delete — and the banner's Restore, which is the same item — off every record such a user could see. No test noticed, because every fixture is an admin. `test/lint/record_edit_gate_test.dart` now fails the build on a bare `can('edit_…')` in an action file; `AuthSession.canEditRecord` is unit-tested arm by arm. A record created on this device and not yet synced is the user's own. Two tables carry no creator locally (bank transactions, bank accounts), so a transaction's gate is approximated with `edit_bank_transaction` *or* `create_bank_transaction`.
- **A create needs its module and `create_<entity>`.** Add To Invoice is offered to a holder of `edit_invoice` *or* `create_invoice`: it edits an invoice not yet chosen, and a creator owns the ones they made.
- **Admin-only on the server, so admin-only here:** a refund (from a payment *or* from its invoice — `RefundPaymentRequest::authorize`), a gateway's writes including Import and Verify (`TestCompanyGatewayRequest`), and a bank account's Archive, Restore and Refresh (`BulkBankIntegrationRequest`, `AdminBankIntegrationRequest`).
- **Converting or unlinking a transaction is gated like its lifecycle.** The server skips a row the user may not edit and still answers 200, so ungated the app reported a conversion that never happened.

**A deleted record's menu is not the same everywhere, and that is inherited rather than designed.** Client, vendor, project, task, expense, recurring expense and product offer only Copy Link, Restore and the pure navigations — the `!isDeleted` grouping admin-portal and React use, which also drops Clone. Payments, the five billing documents, bank accounts, transactions and the settings-hosted records were not trimmed: they hide or disable individual actions, and a few still offer Edit on a deleted record (the server refuses the save with a message the edit screen shows — `docs/sync.md` § A soft-deleted record can't be edited). Edit itself is ungated on the non-billing entities. None of that was changed by the rollout.

What a host supplies: a `RecordScreenController`; a header; a priority list of `EntityQuickAction`s from `quickItemsFor`; the figures for a `StandingCard`; a profile through `RecordProfileLayout` whose cards are each gated on having content; tab ids; and — if it embeds related lists — an `EmbeddedListParentScope` and the count fetchers with the filter each list sends. `test/_support/record_screen_harness.dart` (`recordScreenTest`) mounts the screen over a real `Services` graph and local database for the assembled-screen test each one has.

## Open asks

One per-record summary call on the server would replace nine requests (eight counts and the unpaid-invoices fetch) and make the past-due figure exact rather than proven-or-withheld — `BACKEND.md` § A per-client summary endpoint. The same call for a vendor and a project would replace the page-by-page fetch `RelatedRowsProof` makes to prove a total.

Notes on a recurring expense and on a purchase order are filed under an unrelated expense on the server, and a bank transaction's is accepted and dropped — `BACKEND.md` § Activity notes.

Still open on the client side: the activity feed is built from the route's id, so a record opened as `tmp_…` that syncs while open never loads its comments; cross-record name links are permission-gated on some headers and not others; the tiled actions also appear in the wide action bar (see § A quick-action tile is a second render of an existing action); there is no Refresh item in the `⋮` menu; and a derived total past twenty pages of rows is withheld rather than shown with a caveat.
