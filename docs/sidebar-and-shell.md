# The sidebar and the page shell

Companion to CLAUDE.md § Design system (v2) (the sidebar and shell rules). The sidebar's own chrome — safe areas, ink, the upsell gate, the customisable menu and the pinned rows — plus which widget owns a full-page host. Routing and the back stack live in `docs/architecture.md` § Navigation; this doc does not duplicate them.

## The company switcher is a control, not a label — never gate it away

**The company switcher is a control, not a label — never gate it out of existence.** `CompanyPicker` owns the app's **only** "New company" entry (`new_company` appears nowhere else in `lib/`), so hiding its entry point at `companies.length <= 1` locks a one-company owner out of ever having two — a self-locking failure that looks fine in every test. Issue #16 already removed one such gate (the button went inert below two companies, stranding a user whose roster had wrongly *shrunk*), and issue #104 asked for the same shape again for vertical space. The answer both times is **relocate, never hide**: the single-company mobile drawer drops `SidebarHeader` entirely and re-homes the switcher as `SidebarCompanyFooterAction` (`sidebar_footer_actions.dart`) while Sync moves into the toolbar row. Sign out is the softer half — Settings → User Details has one too, and its `sign_out` search key makes it findable from the settings search and the command palette — but "New company" has no second home, so any future change here must keep a live path to the picker.

## One surface, one safe-area owner — and the owner must be last

**One surface, one safe-area owner — and the owner has to be the *last* thing, not the first.** `InSidebar` wraps its whole `Column` in a single `SafeArea` (`left: false, right: false`) that pays the top and bottom insets for the rail and the drawer alike — horizontal stays deliberately unpaid, since the rail widths are pinned and an iPhone-landscape side inset would crush the 64-px collapsed rail. It used to pass `bottom: false` and cede that edge to `SidebarFooterActions`, which wrapped itself — but the footer is **three** siblings (the icon row, then `TrialFooter`, then `WhiteLabelFooter`), so an inset owned by the first of them padded the *middle* of the footer and left the last card painting inside the Android gesture bar with only its own 12 px below it. On a 24-dp inset that is 8 + 24 + 12 = **44 px of dead space** between the icons and the "Purchase White Label" row, which is what invoiceninja/flutter#124 was filed about; nobody saw it in review because every desktop inset is 0 and the widget tests pump under a zero-padding view. Moving it up is geometrically free — `RenderPadding` deflates the incoming constraints, so every child above keeps its position and the `Expanded` nav list absorbs the inset either way — but two things now depend on placement rather than on a wrapper: **nothing may be mounted below `WhiteLabelFooter`** (it would land in the gesture strip — note the pinned chrome rows added since sit between the nav rule and `SidebarFooterActions`, above all three footer siblings, and pay no inset of their own, so that invariant is untouched), and `bottom: true` also runs `removePadding(removeBottom: true)` over the entire sidebar subtree, so a descendant reading `padding.bottom` sees 0. `test/lint/sidebar_footer_wiring_test.dart` pins both, plus the gate below; it is a **source scan** because a widget test of `InSidebar` deadlocks — `AppDatabase.close()` waits on the saved-views watch releasing its Drift subscriptions on a `Timer.run` that fake time never advances after the last pump, so the file hangs with no output rather than failing (`sidebar_search_box_test.dart` records the same finding). The geometry is covered against replicas in `sidebar_footer_actions_widget_test.dart` and `white_label_footer_test.dart` instead.

## `Ink` is banned — paint a tappable surface on a local `Material`

**`Ink` is banned (`test/lint/no_ink_widget_test.dart`) — paint a tappable surface on a *local* `Material`.** `Ink(decoration:)` registers an `InkDecoration` on the **nearest ancestor** `Material`, and `_RenderInkFeatures.paint` draws every ink feature *before* its child subtree — so any opaque widget in between covers the fill, the border **and** the ripple. `WhiteLabelFooter` was the app's only `Ink`, and its nearest Material was the `Scaffold`'s (the `Drawer`'s in the drawer host) because `InSidebar` has no `Material` of its own; `InSidebar`'s opaque `AnimatedContainer(color: tokens.surface)` sits in that subtree, so the card had rendered as a bare line of unbordered, un-rippling text since the day it shipped — the same #124. The idiom is `Material(color: tokens.surfaceAlt, borderRadius: BorderRadius.circular(InRadii.r2)) > InkWell(borderRadius:) > Container(decoration: border-only)`: the fill rides on the Material above its own ink layer, and the border-only `Container` isn't opaque so the ripple still shows through. `sidebar_search_box.dart`, `sidebar_sync_button.dart` and `company_switcher_button.dart` already do exactly this, which is why *their* fills were never affected. Inside `SidebarNavItem` the idiom now has two users — the grid tile's outline and the pinned chrome rows' selected outline (see the pinned-chrome paragraph) — and both go through the same colourless `Container`, which is what `sidebar_nav_item_test.dart` asserts by checking `decoration.color` is null rather than by looking at the ripple. No `clipBehavior` — `InkWell.borderRadius` already clips the splash and the highlight. **A widget test cannot see this failure**: pumped on its own the widget's ancestor Material *is* directly above it and everything renders, so assert the invariant (the `InkWell`'s nearest `Material` ancestor is a descendant of the card) rather than the appearance.

## A viewport can be too short for optional chrome

**A viewport can be too *short* for optional chrome, and `Breakpoints` cannot answer that.** `InSidebar` drops the white-label upsell below `_kUpsellMinHeight` (480, Material 3's compact-height class) when `Env.isTouchPrimary` — a landscape phone, where the sidebar's fixed chrome already costs ~243 px of a 412-px viewport and leaves the nav list about 3½ of its ~12 rows. Three things are deliberate. The constant is **local, not a `Breakpoints` member**: that class holds *width* thresholds, so `height < wide` reads as nonsense at the call site, and `reportTableMedium` is the precedent for minting a separate constant at the same value rather than reusing one. **480, not 600**: every phone **in landscape** is below it (iPhone SE 375, Pixel 8 412, iPhone 15 Pro Max 430), so it clears the whole reported population, while 480–600 is mostly deliberately-short desktop windows (a half-screen 1080p snap is 1920×540) — the self-hosted desktop users the CTA exists to convert, with ~300 px of nav list to spare. Neither half of that gate is airtight and the code says so: `Env.isTouchPrimary` is **not** a proxy for "handset" (it is true for every `TargetPlatform.android`, so a ChromeOS container, Samsung DeX or Android desktop-mode window at 1400×450 does lose the card), and Android vertical split-screen can hand a real phone ~450–540 px and so keep it. Both are accepted — rare, resizable, and the offer stays one click away in Settings → Account Management → Plan — but don't tighten the rule on the strength of either claim without re-checking it. And **only the upsell is gated** — `TrialFooter` is not, because an expiring trial is time-critical and has no second home, whereas the standing offer is always reachable at Settings → Account Management → Plan (`showWhiteLabelCard`). `MediaQuery.sizeOf`, not a `LayoutBuilder`: the rail's own box shrinks by `viewInsets.bottom` when a keyboard opens in the *content* pane, which would drop and restore the card mid-typing. The price is that the gate reads the **window**, which the *drawer* host is not — it sits in an `Expanded` under `OfflineBanner` and above `_DebugPanelBand`, so either of those can make the box shorter than the number the gate saw. Cosmetic, and confined to the narrow host where the gate should never fire. The decision is a pure function, `sidebarShowsUpsell(touch:height:)`, so the threshold is unit-tested rather than pinned by scanning the source for its expression — which is how `sidebar_search_box_test.dart` once went red on a rename that changed nothing.

## The main menu is a preference, and its two halves fail oppositely

**The main menu is a *preference*, and the two halves of that fail in opposite directions.** #125 asked for the dashboard's quick-action pills inside the hamburger menu ("increase information density") and for per-user ordering ("Quotes should be up with Invoices") — the third density complaint from the same Android tester after #101 and #104. Both live in one device-local blob, `nav_state.sidebar_menu_json` (schema v8), behind `SidebarMenuController`; the pure parts — the `"<id>|<1|0>"` encoding, and `resolveMenuEntries` — sit in `lib/domain/sidebar_menu.dart`, which imports nothing. Defaults are today's behaviour exactly (list layout, registry order, nothing hidden), and the column stays **null** until something changes, so "never customised" and "reset" are one state rather than two. Six things are load-bearing, and only one of them is visible in review. **The griddable block stops before Outbox.** It is Dashboard + the entity rows + Reports + Activity; Saved views keeps arbitrary user-typed names that would ellipsize to nothing in an 80-px tile, and **Outbox is the only row in the sidebar whose visibility resolves asynchronously** (`hideWhenZero`, inside a `StreamBuilder`) — every entry above it is gated synchronously at build time, so no grid cell can collapse to zero and strand an empty column, which is #124's lesson made unreachable by construction rather than re-fixed. `test/lint/sidebar_menu_wiring_test.dart` pins that (a source scan: `InSidebar` still cannot be pumped). **`kSidebarMenuMinTileWidth` is 66 and is not free to pick** — the sidebar has exactly two content widths (256 in the 280-px drawer, 208 on the rail, each after the list's 12+12), and at **84** the *drawer* falls to two columns at normal text scale, which is barely denser than the list it replaced and is not the grid the issue asked for; at **76** it is three until the user picks Large, which is an ordinary setting, not an edge case. 66 holds three through Large and yields at Extra Large. Nothing about that is visible on a reviewer's screen, so `sidebar_nav_grid_test.dart` asserts the table cell by cell. **`resolveMenuEntries` splices a *new* destination in at its default index** rather than appending it: without that, an entity shipped in a later release would be missing from the menu of every user who had ever customised theirs, and they would have no way to notice. Same splice `EntityColumnPickerSheet._applied` uses for an unrenderable column. **The sheet resolves against every destination; the sidebar resolves against the module-enabled subset** — and `setEntries` therefore has exactly one caller. Writing back a list resolved from the sidebar's narrower view erases the stored position of every entity whose module the company happens to have off, silently, for only those users. **Hiding a row leaks a live Drift query unless the badge cache is told.** `_noteBadgeMode` fires only for a row that renders, which was airtight while the only way to lose a row was a module switched off (such a row never reaches `_cachedBadge`, so it never opens a query); a hidden row *did* render, opened its query, and then stopped — hence `_noteRenderedEntities`. And **the tile's fill rides on the `Material`, its border on a non-opaque `Container` inside the `InkWell`** — the `Ink` ban's idiom — while the one slot beside the icon carries `trailing` if there is one and the count badge otherwise: `trailing` is Reports' Pro lock, the only pre-tap signal that destination is gated, and the badge keeps its *number* rather than degrading to the rail's dot, which exists only because 64 px has no room for digits. An **enabled** tile also carries a full-label `Tooltip` (a disabled one keeps its `coming_soon` message, which returns first), since it ellipsizes a long single word ("Transactions" past Normal, far sooner in German) and has no width to grow into — and that tooltip must stay **`TooltipTriggerMode.manual`**: on its default, `RawTooltip` registers a `LongPressGestureRecognizer` from a `Listener` *below* `SidebarNavItem`, so it enters the arena ahead of the `_BadgeModeMenuTarget` wrapped around the outside, wins the same 500 ms deadline, and leaves every tile answering a long press with its own label instead of the counter menu — on touch, which is the grid's entire audience and has no right-click to fall back on. Hover is unaffected (trigger mode "does not affect mouse devices").

## Settings and Outbox are chrome, pinned below the nav list

**Settings and Outbox are chrome, not destinations — they are pinned below the nav list, and their selected state drops the accent.** #131 asked for Settings "towards the bottom" of the grid menu and for its blue to go. The blue was never a Settings style: `SidebarNavItem` paints `accentSoft` + `accent` + `accentInk` for whichever row matches the active branch, and the reporter had screenshotted the menu while standing in Settings — but the position complaint was exact. `_buildItems` emitted the grid, then `_SavedViewsSection`, then Settings; with no saved views that middle entry collapses to `SizedBox.shrink()` **and takes the 8-px spacer above Settings with it**, so the tile block ran straight into a full-width row with hundreds of pixels of dead space below it. `_pinnedChromeRows` now mounts both rows between the nav rule and `SidebarFooterActions`, in every layout. Six things are load-bearing, plus two smaller traps at the end. **Below the existing rule, never above it** — the scroll viewport already clips there, so a half-scrolled row is cut by a crisp line and the chrome sits in the band beyond; above it, a clipped row would abut the Settings row with nothing between them, and the only fix is a third hairline, which on a 232-px rail makes the bottom read as three regions one of which is 44 px tall. **A structural sibling of the `Expanded`, never a `Spacer` inside it** — the rows must not scroll away, which `SliverFillRemaining(hasScrollBody: false)` still permits, and *both* intrinsic-sizing recipes are unavailable anyway: `SidebarNavGrid` wraps its runs in a `LayoutBuilder`, which throws on an intrinsic query in debug and answers 0 in release, so an `IntrinsicHeight` over the nav list would either crash or silently mis-size. **Outbox goes above Settings**, reversing the order these two shipped in: the `Expanded` absorbs every height change, so the block's bottom edge is nailed to the footer and its top edge is what travels — with Settings last it never moves, and the other way round every queued mutation shoves it up 44 px under the user's thumb. The `AnimatedSize` around them is for the same event and is not decoration (the header's deliberate *absence* of one is a different case — that 1→2 company transition never happens on screen; this one happens all day). **`_pinnedChromeRows` must be defined between `_buildItems` and `_buildMenuEntries`**, because the older lint slices the source between `_buildMenuEntries` and `_entityNav` and fails on the literal `hideWhenZero`, which the Outbox row carries — defined after it, a pure move turns a grid lint red with a message about grid cells. **The block deliberately sits outside the `sidebarMenu` / `sidebarBadgeModes` / `_company` / `_activeView` builders** — none of their inputs reach `_fixedNav` — which also stops the Outbox `StreamBuilder`'s element being re-seated on every list↔grid flip; the trap is that the moment Settings becomes menu-customisable it stops repainting on that preference, symptom "the toggle does nothing until you navigate away". And **the neutral selected state is carried by ink, not by the fill**: `surfaceAlt` on `surface` runs 1.05-1.10:1 across the six palettes and `borderStrong` 1.39-1.70:1, while the accent state it replaces carries only 1.14-1.24:1 of *luminance* and works on hue — so both ink steps go two notches (`ink2`→`ink` label, `ink3`→**`ink`** icon, 3.5-4.5:1). The single figures quoted in the code are the default dark preset's (Carbon: 1.07, 1.42, 1.24, 3.68); the argument holds in all six, which is the point of the ranges, which is also the only cue left on the collapsed 64-px rail, where there is no label, no weight and no badge. User overrides cut both ways here and neither direction was free: `CustomToken` is `{background, surface, ink, border}`, so `surface` is overridable while `surfaceAlt` and `borderStrong` are not — a custom surface near the dark fill would erase a *fill*-borne state with nothing to re-derive it, unlike `accentSoft`, which `buildInTheme` re-derives from the per-user `accent_color`. But `ink` is overridable too and `applyTo` leaves `ink2` / `ink3` at the preset's values, so an ink override does **not** re-derive its family the way an accent override does: a custom ink close to `ink3` flattens this state on the collapsed rail, where the icon is the only cue. Accepted — such an ink also greys every primary string in the app, so it is a visible, self-inflicted choice rather than a silent trap — but do not repeat the claim that the neutral state is override-proof. Two smaller traps: the outline is drawn in **both** states, `Colors.transparent` at rest, because a `Container` border folds `EdgeInsets.all(1)` into its child and an outline added only on selection makes a *pointer* row 32→34 px the moment you open Settings — touch's 44-px floor swallows it, so it is invisible on a phone and in every touch test; and the count badge must pass `active: widget.active && !_neutralSelected`, or `SidebarBadge`'s neutral tone flips to `accent` digits on a `surface` chip — on the one row this fix exists to de-blue. The chosen branch has its own accepted cost: the chip's fill then equals the row's, so the count reads on the fill rather than in a filled chip (it keeps its own `border` outline, and the digits stay `ink3`). Widening `colorsFor` instead would reach the Sidebar-counters live preview in Device Settings, for a row the user is already standing on. Only one row in the whole sidebar is ever active — an entity row needs `activeViewId == null`, a saved-view row needs a match, and a fixed branch forces `activeViewId` null — so the accent and neutral treatments can never appear together. `test/lint/sidebar_menu_wiring_test.dart` pins the placement and **counts** the two `SidebarNavSelection.neutral` opt-ins, which is the only assertion in the suite that can see a half-done job: `hideWhenZero` means a developer with an empty outbox never renders that row, so a missing `selection:` there is green, silent and correct-looking on screen.

## On a narrow viewport every full-page host must bring its own `Scaffold`

**On a narrow viewport nothing above a screen owns a `Scaffold`, so every full-page host must bring its own — and a missing one costs keyboard avoidance, silently.** `ScaffoldWithNav`'s narrow (<600) branch is a deliberate passthrough ("each top-level screen renders its own Scaffold… avoids `Scaffold.of(context)` ambiguity"); its wide branch has one. `Scaffold` is the **only** thing that *resizes a screen* for `MediaQuery.viewInsets.bottom` — everything else that copes with the keyboard (the toast host, the bottom sheets, the date-range popover) pads by it by hand. Every `Scaffold(` in `lib/` relies on the default `resizeToAvoidBottomInset: true`; only the narrow pane below writes it out, because there it is the whole point rather than incidental. Miss it and the screen keeps its full window height under the on-screen keyboard, so `EditableText`'s caret reveal asks the ancestor `Scrollable` to make the caret visible, finds it already inside a viewport that extends *behind* the keyboard, and never scrolls: a field low in a long form is simply covered. That was invoiceninja/flutter#105 — the `MasterDetailLayout` narrow pane wrapped itself in a bare `Material`, and since the list's Scaffold is a **sibling** in that Stack (`Offstage`) rather than an ancestor, every entity edit / detail screen on a phone had none. It now builds a `Scaffold`; the wide branch deliberately does not (the shell's Scaffold owns the inset there, and the 600–1024 band goes through the narrow pane *under* that shell — nested Scaffolds can't double-inset, since the outer one hands its body `removeViewInsets(removeBottom: true)`). A route-by-route audit found no other gap: auth, dashboard, reports, activity, outbox, every entity **list**, every `/settings/**` screen and every `Navigator.push` destination build their own — bar `showCascadeFullScreenPreview` (`cascade_tabbed_settings_shell.dart`), which pushes a caller-supplied builder with no chrome of its own and is only safe today because every caller happens to bring a Scaffold and none has a text input. Two corollaries: a `Scaffold` body reads `viewInsets.bottom` as **0**, so padding by it in there is dead code — `templates_reminders_body.dart` has one such line that has never done anything, and note an `OverlayPortal` popover resolves inherited widgets from the *portal's* position, so a picker's open-direction math sees that zero too; and **`showModalBottomSheet` lifts nothing by itself** — a sheet with a text input must pad by `MediaQuery.viewInsetsOf(ctx).bottom` in its `builder`, which also fixes a `heightFactor` / `maxHeight` budget otherwise measured against the unshrunk screen. `line_item_picker_sheet.dart` is the reference; five sheets had forgotten it, two of them autofocusing their field.
